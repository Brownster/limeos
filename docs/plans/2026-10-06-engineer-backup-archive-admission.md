## Deliverables

1. Define closed admission policy, archive entry facts and a deterministic restore manifest. A trusted registry maps managed resource IDs and relative paths to future destinations. Archive contents cannot select arbitrary host paths. Bind the accepted manifest to the full inspected archive digest, format, policy revision, entry count, byte totals and per-file checksums. Never return a usable plan after a partial inspection.
2. Implement a bounded streaming inspector for legacy `.tar.gz` and `.tar.zst` archives. The frozen helper creates those formats; its restore currently invokes tar over `/`. Inspect the actual effective entry names and metadata, including supported tar extension records, rather than trusting a filename suffix or a caller-provided entry list. Reject malformed, truncated or unsupported constructs with explicit findings.
3. Enforce finite policy limits on compressed input, all decompressed bytes including headers/metadata, individual files, entries, names/path depth, metadata and decoder memory. Count actual streamed bytes with checked arithmetic, including skipped entries. Reject oversized declarations before allocation. Stop reads at the limit, honor cancellation and bound work; do not load an entire archive or decompressed stream into memory. The trusted policy owns limits; archive metadata cannot relax them.
4. Reject absolute paths, `..` components, unsafe/ambiguous names, duplicate destinations, file/directory collisions and anything outside the managed registry. Preserve literal case-sensitive paths. Initially admit regular files and directories only; reject symlinks, hardlinks, device entries, FIFOs, sockets and sparse files. Document this supported subset and any legitimate legacy link/sparse use that needs later support. Archive ownership, setuid/setgid bits and extension metadata cannot authorize privileged installation; future ownership comes from trusted managed-resource policy.
5. Provide a fixture matrix that exercises the real decoding/inspection path. Include traversal, absolute paths, link chains and escapes, device files, duplicate names, parent-file collisions, extension-header path overrides, malformed sizes/checksums, truncated streams, huge metadata, entry floods, high expansion and arithmetic overflow. Include valid representative archives and quota boundary cases. Use small fixtures and low test quotas so a decompression-bomb regression remains bounded. Tests must prove rejection produces no accepted manifest and changes no managed destination.
6. Document the integration interface: inspection takes a bounded archive reader and trusted policy; admission returns a digest-bound manifest or typed rejection. The future executor must inspect an immutable staged copy, revalidate its identity/digest, resolve destinations through protected descriptors and keep recovery evidence. List these requirements explicitly rather than treating a manifest as permission to restore.

Legacy tar members can contain relative representations of former absolute source paths. Map those only through an explicit trusted legacy mapping and report unsupported sources. A broad `/etc`, `/var` or `/home` allowlist would recreate the old authority. Use synthetic archives and sanitized source fixtures; include no real credentials, databases or `.env` contents in commits.
