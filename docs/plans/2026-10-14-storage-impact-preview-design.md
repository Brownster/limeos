## Choice and boundary

Derive a bounded selection preview from all five original typed inputs and the
existing checked configured graph. This reuses the frozen pool/protection/share
rules and preserves full container/source/destination relationships. Selecting
rows from imported graph JSON would lose validation and age checks; materializing
operational claims now would depend on incomplete physical and stack evidence.

Accept one to 32 complete expected StorageDevice values. Reject duplicate,
unknown or changed selections, including UUID, role, filesystem, mountpoint and
serial differences. Validate every configuration and the original Docker age,
including invalid unused/disabled rows. Preserve the graph's original issue time,
full expected metadata, flags, branch order, unmapped shares and numeric ownership.

## Selected dependents and related resources

Selected graph rows identify possible dependent containers, shares and pools.
Stopped/read-only containers and disabled/read-only configuration remain resource
candidates. Running dependent snapshots form a deduplicated review list; no stack
ownership is inferred from container IDs. Names are exact, component-aware and
case-sensitive according to the existing planners.

Touching a pool includes its pool ID, mount root and every configured member
device/mount in the resource candidates. Touching any SnapRAID drive includes one
global protection subject and every configured protection drive's device/mount.
This permits future coordination of a pool or global protection operation without
inventing individual-drive execution. Related member devices are distinct from
selected devices: they do not become unmount targets, and their direct consumers
or other pools do not become stop candidates merely because they share membership.
Shared backing, symlinks and aliases require later physical closure.

Use a typed serialize-only resource enum for device IDs, mount names, pool IDs,
global SnapRAID, exact share names and full container resources. It is independent
of Store claim keys. Deduplicate before count growth, sort deterministically, and
refuse a 129th distinct candidate under a fixed 128-subject limit. Reuse the
graph's existing global 512-route ceiling. Bound the complete borrowed serialized
preview to 256 KiB with a counting writer; a byte/count refusal returns no partial
preview. No caller-selected cap or JSON authority import is exposed.

Every result lists unresolved retained/physical evidence, actual pool/protection/
share state, stack ownership, complete claims/approvals and effect-time checks.
Empty selection/dependent lists cannot become safe-unmount authority. This is an
unregistered domain function, with no CLI/RPC/route/schema/service/package change
and no change to existing graph or combined-collector formats or service caps.
